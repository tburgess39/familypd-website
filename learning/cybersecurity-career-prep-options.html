Cybersecurity Career Prep Options

There is no single correct route into cybersecurity. Compare several options, then build a plan that fits the learner’s age, schedule, resources, interests, and goals.

Compare optionsStart with CTE

Build the Foundation First

Cybersecurity depends on understanding computers, operating systems, networks, identities, files, troubleshooting, and professional ethics. A strong plan builds those foundations instead of jumping directly to advanced security tools.

1

High-School Career & Technical Education

Best for students who can build skills through a sequenced school program.
School-based

What It Can Include

Course sequences, hands-on labs, industry credentials, career and technical student organizations, work-based learning, capstones, and sometimes college credit.

What Families Should Check

Which schools offer the program, how students enter, application deadlines, transportation, prerequisites, course sequence, credentials, and transfer options.

Explore the CTE Page

Use the FamilyPD checklist and open official CCSD and Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools CTE resources.

Explore CTE programs
2

Nevada Partners

One workforce-connected option for learners in Southern Nevada.
Workforce program

Foundation

Begins with IT essentials so learners understand hardware, operating systems, support, networking, troubleshooting, and security basics.

Stacked Learning

Continues through networking, Linux, and security so each course adds another technical layer.

Review This Option

See the current FamilyPD overview, then confirm enrollment, eligibility, scheduling, and course details directly with Nevada Partners.

Review Nevada Partners
3

College, Community College, and Microcredentials

Useful for learners who want academic credit, structured instruction, labs, or a broader degree plan.
Postsecondary

Certificates

Shorter programs may focus on support, networking, cloud, Linux, or cybersecurity and can sometimes stack toward a degree.

Degrees

Associate and bachelor’s programs may include general education, technical courses, projects, internships, and broader career preparation.

Questions to Ask

Is the program current? Are labs hands-on? Which certifications or internships are supported? Do credits transfer? What is the full cost?

4

Certifications, Competitions, and Guided Self-Study

Flexible options that work best when paired with real practice and documented evidence.
Flexible

Certifications

Credentials such as A+, Network+, Linux+, and Security+ can organize foundational study. Always verify the current exam version and objectives.

Competitions and Labs

CyberPatriot, National Cyber League, CTFs, practice ranges, home labs, and portfolios can help learners apply knowledge safely.

Self-Directed Learning

Books, official documentation, guided courses, videos, projects, and study groups can fill gaps—but learners still need structure, feedback, and ethical boundaries.
