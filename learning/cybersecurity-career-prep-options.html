Cybersecurity Career Prep Options

There is no single correct route into cybersecurity. Compare several options, then build a plan that fits the learner’s age, schedule, resources, interests, and goals.

Compare optionsStart with CTE

Build the Foundation First

Cybersecurity depends on understanding computers, operating systems, networks, identities, files, troubleshooting, and professional ethics. A strong plan builds those foundations instead of jumping directly to advanced security tools.

1

High-School Career & Technical Education

Best for students who can build skills through a sequenced school program.
School-based

What It Can Include

Course sequences, hands-on labs, industry credentials, career and technical student organizations, work-based learning, capstones, and sometimes college credit.

What Families Should Check

Which schools offer the program, how students enter, application deadlines, transportation, prerequisites, course sequence, credentials, and transfer options.

Explore the CTE Page

Use the FamilyPD checklist and open official CCSD and Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools CTE resources.

Explore CTE programs
2

Nevada Partners

One workforce-connected option for learners in Southern Nevada.
Workforce program

Foundation

Begins with IT essentials so learners understand hardware, operating systems, support, networking, troubleshooting, and security basics.

Stacked Learning

Continues through computer and network forensics, Linux, and security preparation so each course adds another technical layer.

Review This Option

Review the FamilyPD overview, then confirm current eligibility, application periods, scheduling, and course details directly with Nevada Partners.

Review Nevada Partners
3

College, Community College, and Microcredentials

Compare structured programs, transfer routes, technical certificates, and hands-on cybersecurity options.
Postsecondary

College of Southern Nevada (CSN)

CSN offers cybersecurity pathways in compliance, digital forensics, and network security, plus networking and computer science options. Its cybersecurity center also supports competitions, labs, workforce connections, and transfer planning.

Explore CSN Cybersecurity

Coastal Carolina Community College

Coastal Carolina Community College in Onslow County offers Information Technology pathways in Information Systems and Systems Security. Coursework includes hardware, operating systems, networking, Linux, security, digital forensics, and ethical hacking.

Explore Coastal Carolina

University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV)

UNLV offers undergraduate and graduate cybersecurity options, including an interdisciplinary online B.S. in Cybersecurity and a Computer Science Information Assurance concentration, along with labs, research, and cyber-range experiences.

Explore UNLV Programs
Compare before enrolling: Ask about transfer credit, total cost, scheduling, lab access, internship support, certification alignment, and whether the program matches the learner’s current foundation and career goals.
4

Certifications, Competitions, and Guided Self-Study

Flexible options that work best when paired with real practice and documented evidence.
Flexible

Certifications

Credentials such as A+, Network+, Linux+, and Security+ can organize foundational study. Network+ is especially useful because cybersecurity depends on understanding how devices communicate, how traffic moves, how services use ports, and how networks are secured and troubleshot.

Explore CompTIA Network+

Competitions and Labs

CyberPatriot, National Cyber League, CTFs, practice ranges, home labs, and portfolios can help learners apply knowledge safely.

Self-Directed Learning

Books, official documentation, guided courses, videos, projects, and study groups can fill gaps—but learners still need structure, feedback, and ethical boundaries.
