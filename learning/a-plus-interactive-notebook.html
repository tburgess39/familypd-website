A+ Study • Make • Review

Build an A+ interactive notebook you will actually use.

Do not just copy notes. Build a hands-on study tool with drawings, printed images, labels, flaps, mini-books, cable samples, paper mechanisms, memory tricks, and your own explanations. The goal is to make difficult A+ concepts easier to see, touch, remember, and review later.

Go to the A+ courseBrowse notebook images
You do not need a printer. Use the FamilyPD images as references. You may print them, draw your own version, use another accurate image, cut pictures from magazines or old catalogs, or safely attach small real items such as short pieces of retired cable. Your notebook should show what you understand, not how well you can decorate.

How to build it

Use a simple repeatable process.

1. Learn first

Read or explore the concept before building the page. Identify the few facts you truly need to remember.

2. Make it visual

Draw, print, trace, cut out, label, fold, or attach something that represents the concept.

3. Add your words

Write what the part, command, protocol, or process does in plain language. Avoid copying a paragraph you do not understand.

4. Make it interactive

Use a flap, wheel, slider, pocket, matching piece, hidden answer, lift-up label, or removable card when it helps learning.

5. Add a memory hook

Add a short analogy, sketch, acronym, comparison, color cue, or phrase that helps you retrieve the idea later.

6. Review it

Cover the answers and test yourself. If the page cannot help you practice, improve it before moving on.

Build ideas

Turn A+ topics into things you can manipulate.

TopicNotebook ideaWhat to practice
PC hardwareDraw or print a motherboard. Add lift-up labels for CPU, RAM, storage connectors, expansion slots, power connectors, and ports.Name the component, identify it, and explain its job.
CPU + RAMMake a CPU mini-book and RAM flap. Trace how active data moves between storage, RAM, and CPU.Cores, threads, clock speed, sockets, RAM types, capacity, and temporary vs. persistent storage.
StorageCreate a pocket with removable HDD, SATA SSD, M.2 SATA, and NVMe cards.Interface, form factor, speed, capacity, and best-use comparisons.
Cables + portsAttach safe, short pieces of retired cable or draw connector ends. Make a flip book with the port hidden under each flap.Recognize connectors and match them to their purpose.
NetworkingBuild a small paper network. Move a packet card from device → switch/AP → router → destination.IP, MAC, ports, protocols, devices, wired/wireless, and troubleshooting.
Operating systemsUse tabs or mini-cards for Windows, Linux, macOS, mobile OSs, file systems, commands, and utilities.Recognize tools, commands, features, and troubleshooting choices.
SecurityBuild a CIA triad wheel, authentication flap, malware matchbook, and physical-security mini-cards.Threat recognition, controls, permissions, authentication, and safe response.
TroubleshootingCreate a six-step troubleshooting accordion. Put a symptom on the front and likely checks underneath.Use a process instead of guessing.

Use what you have

Low-cost materials work well.

Paper mechanisms

  • Lift-up flaps
  • Foldables and accordions
  • Spinning wheels with a paper fastener
  • Pull tabs and sliders
  • Mini-books
  • Pockets with removable cards

Real-world materials

  • Small pieces of retired Ethernet or USB cable
  • Old connector ends that are safe and clean
  • Packaging labels and hardware advertisements
  • Photos of old PC components
  • Device manuals or recycled catalogs

Your own visuals

  • Hand-drawn diagrams
  • Icons and symbols
  • Memory cartoons
  • Color-coded arrows
  • Numbered component maps
  • Before/after troubleshooting sketches

Safety: Do not dismantle powered devices, open a power supply, attach sharp electronic parts, or use damaged batteries. Use only clean, disconnected, safe materials.

Suggested notebook flow

Build in the same order that the concepts connect.

Foundation

Certification roadmap → computer anatomy → CPU/RAM → storage/cables → mobile devices.

Connect

Networking → virtualization/cloud → operating systems → commands and utilities.

Protect + repair

Security → troubleshooting → operational procedures → mixed review and exam-prep pages.

Make it useful for exam day

Your notebook is a study tool, not a scrapbook.

Do

  • Write short explanations in your own words.
  • Include acronyms and what they mean.
  • Practice identifying parts without labels.
  • Add mistakes you keep making.
  • Connect related concepts with arrows.
  • Return to old pages and improve them.

Avoid

  • Filling every inch just to make it look busy.
  • Using tiny text you will not reread.
  • Copying facts without understanding them.
  • Adding mechanisms that hide the important information.
  • Spending more time decorating than practicing.

Quick review routine

Look: Study the visual.

Cover: Hide the labels or answers.

Recall: Say or write what you remember.

Check: Reveal the answer.

Repair: Add a memory trick where you struggled.
