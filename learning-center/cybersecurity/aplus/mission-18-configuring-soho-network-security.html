18.1 • Threat recognition and response

A vulnerability is a weakness, a threat is a potential cause of harm, and risk combines likelihood and impact. Phishing may arrive by email, text, or voice; an evil twin imitates a trusted Wi-Fi network. Verify requests through a known channel and report suspicious messages. Applications reduce injection risk by separating commands from data and rendering untrusted text safely.

18.2 • Wireless authentication

Use WPA3-Personal where supported; WPA2 with AES is a compatibility option where policy permits. Personal mode uses a shared credential; enterprise authentication can assign identities through 802.1X with a RADIUS backend and certificate validation. RADIUS commonly supports network access authentication, TACACS+ is used for device administration, and Kerberos uses tickets for domain services. Put visitors and untrusted IoT devices on separated networks.

Revoke one Wi-Fi identity

A departing staff member should lose wireless access while coworkers continue working.

Try either action to see its effect.

Study tip. Compare enrollment and revocation, not only connection setup.

18.3 • Router hardening and physical controls

Inventory the router, update supported firmware through its approved process, and replace default administration credentials. Restrict management to trusted interfaces, disable unused UPnP and inbound mappings, and review separate guest or IoT network rules. A screened subnet separates public services from internal systems. Locks, badges, lighting, cameras, and alarms address physical access and complement network controls.

Verify guest isolation

The router change should let guests reach the internet while blocking the staff printer.

Try either action to see its effect.

Study tip. A complete firewall verification includes both allowed and blocked traffic.

An apparent supervisor requests a password reset by text and supplies a callback number. What should you do?
Explanation

Independent verification prevents the sender from controlling both channels.

Which design lets individual staff lose wireless access when they depart without replacing a shared Wi-Fi password?
Explanation

Individual credentials can be revoked while other accounts continue to work.

A guest device can reach an office printer that should be private. Which change addresses the exposure?
Explanation

Segmentation constrains guest access at the network boundary.

Which test gives the strongest evidence the isolation policy works?
Explanation

Test permitted and prohibited connections from the relevant networks.
