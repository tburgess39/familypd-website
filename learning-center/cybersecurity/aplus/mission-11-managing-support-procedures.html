11.1 • Documentation & ticketing

A technician needs repeatable processes, clear evidence, and a way to hand work to another technician.

SOP — Standard Operating Procedure

Step-by-step approved instructions for a repeatable task. Use a checklist when steps must be followed consistently; use guidelines when judgment is needed.

Check your understanding

A technician repeatedly forgets the final verification step. What should be improved?

The SOP checklist — A standard procedure should include verification and documentation.

SLA — Service Level Agreement

An agreement defining measurable service commitments, such as response targets, resolution targets, and availability. These are not interchangeable.

Check your understanding

A provider promises a reply within 2 hours. Which metric is this?

Response time — A response target is not a promise that the problem is fixed.

Ticket lifecycle

A ticket captures the issue, impact, category, priority, owner, evidence, attempted fixes, customer updates, resolution, and confirmation. A request is not necessarily an incident.

Check your understanding

A user asks for a new account. Which category fits best?

Service request — Provisioning a new account is a planned request.

Priority vs severity

Severity describes technical impact; priority determines work order using impact, urgency, and organizational rules. A single-person outage may be urgent if business-critical.

Check your understanding

A payroll outage affects everyone. What should rise?

Priority — Widespread impact and time sensitivity justify faster handling.

Escalation tiers

Tier 0 is self-service; Tier 1 initial diagnosis; Tier 2 specialized support; Tier 3 advanced engineering/vendor-level work. Follow local policy rather than assuming every company uses identical tiers.

Check your understanding

A technician lacks permission to change a server. What next?

Escalate appropriately — Do not bypass authorization.

Knowledge base & lessons learned

Knowledge-base articles document symptoms, prerequisites, steps, results, and when to escalate. A lessons-learned review identifies changes to prevent recurrence.

Check your understanding

A fix works repeatedly. What should be updated?

Knowledge base — Record reusable troubleshooting steps and limitations.

AUP — Acceptable Use Policy

Defines acceptable and prohibited behavior on organizational devices and networks. Follow privacy, access, and retention policies when writing tickets.

Check your understanding

A ticket contains a password. What should you do?

Remove the secret — Never expose credentials in routine ticket notes.

11.2 • Communication & customer care

Technical accuracy is not enough: the customer must understand what will happen, when, and what is safe.

Active listening

Let the customer describe symptoms, paraphrase the concern, ask for confirmation, and avoid assumptions or blame.

Check your understanding

A customer is frustrated. What comes first?

Listen and clarify — Acknowledge the concern before troubleshooting.

Open vs closed questions

Open-ended questions uncover the story: “What changed before this started?” Closed-ended questions confirm specifics: “Does it happen on Wi-Fi?”

Check your understanding

You know little about a problem. Which question type first?

Open-ended — Begin broadly, then narrow with confirmation questions.

Expectation setting

Explain next steps, realistic timeframes, impact, and updates. Never guarantee a fix before diagnosing the cause.

Check your understanding

A repair may take hours. What should you provide?

Realistic update — Give a supported timeline and follow-up plan.

Professional conduct

Arrive prepared, avoid distractions and jargon, respect culture and accessibility needs, and do not view unrelated personal files.

Check your understanding

You see private photos during support. What should you do?

Avoid access — Only inspect material needed and authorized for the task.

De-escalation

Stay calm, avoid arguing, state boundaries, and seek supervisor/security help for threats according to policy.

Check your understanding

A caller becomes threatening. What next?

Follow safety policy — Escalate unsafe behavior rather than continuing an abusive exchange.

11.3 • Operating systems, file systems & lifecycle

An operating system manages hardware and applications. A file system organizes data on storage. Neither term means the same thing.

Windows and macOS

Windows supports many vendors and enterprise tools. macOS is Apple’s desktop OS. Windows 11 compatibility includes supported CPU, TPM 2.0, Secure Boot capability, memory and storage requirements.

Check your understanding

A PC lacks TPM 2.0. What should be checked?

OS requirements — Verify vendor and Microsoft compatibility before upgrading.

Linux, UNIX and ChromeOS

Linux distributions package a kernel with user-space tools; UNIX is a related family with its own history. ChromeOS is Linux-based and emphasizes managed, web-centered workflows.

Check your understanding

A Linux device has no desktop. Can it still run?

Yes, via shell — A graphical desktop is not required for Linux.

Mobile operating systems

iOS runs on iPhone, iPadOS on iPad, and Android on many vendors’ devices. Check app permissions, update support, and device-specific management.

Check your understanding

An app fails after an OS update. What check first?

App compatibility — Check supported OS versions and updates.

NTFS, FAT32, exFAT

NTFS supports Windows permissions and journaling; FAT32 has a 4 GiB minus 1 byte maximum individual file size; exFAT supports large files and broad device interoperability.

Check your understanding

A 6 GB video will not copy to FAT32. Why?

File size limit — Use an appropriate file system after backing up data.

ext4, XFS, APFS, ReFS

ext4 and XFS are common Linux file systems; APFS is Apple’s modern file system; ReFS emphasizes resilience for supported Windows workloads. Features vary by version and platform.

Check your understanding

A macOS startup disk uses which modern format?

APFS — APFS is the normal modern Apple file system.

End of life vs support

End of support means vendor updates and assistance may stop. Unsupported software increases risk and may violate organizational policy.

Check your understanding

An OS no longer gets security fixes. What next?

Plan migration — Document risk and move to supported software.

🎮 Help desk triage: assess before acting

Read the reported symptoms. Choose a priority and the safest first step. Priority is based on impact and urgency, not just the number of affected people. These are training scenarios; real organizations use their own priority matrix.

Choose a report, priority, and next step.
See priority reasoning and escalation guidance

Campus-wide outage: high priority because impact is broad; confirm and escalate under incident procedures. Mouse request: low priority when work can continue, and follow normal service-request fulfillment. Payroll: high priority because the deadline creates business urgency even though one workstation is affected. Confirm symptoms before escalating. Do not claim a specific SLA without organizational policy.

💾 USB copy troubleshooting: test the evidence

Simulation assumes a healthy 32 GiB drive, enough free space, and a single file. Choose the computer OS, drive format, and file size. Results distinguish file-size limits from OS compatibility. Never reformat a real drive without a verified backup.

PBQ-style troubleshooting checklist
  1. Check the exact error and available free space.
  2. Inspect the file system and size of the individual file.
  3. Confirm the operating system can write to that format.
  4. Back up data before considering reformatting; test and verify the final copy.

Exam tips & notebook suggestions

  • Read the last sentence first: Is the question asking for the first, best, or next action?
  • Document before escalating: Include symptoms, scope, changes, checks, results, and who owns the next step.
  • Do not confuse response and resolution targets. Also distinguish severity from priority.
  • Know the file-size clue: a file larger than 4 GiB fails on FAT32 even when plenty of free space remains.
  • Notebook idea: Create a ticket template and a simple escalation flowchart; add a table comparing NTFS, exFAT, FAT32, ext4, XFS, APFS, and ReFS.
  • Notebook idea: Write two examples of professional customer updates and a one-page open-vs-closed-question guide.
  • Retrieval practice: Close your notes and explain the difference between SOP, SLA, AUP, knowledge base, and lessons learned.
