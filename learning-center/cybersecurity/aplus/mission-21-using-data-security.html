Using Data Security

Choose recoverable backups and handle sensitive records according to policy.

21.1 • Backup methods and recovery

Full backups copy all selected data. Incrementals capture changes since the most recent backup; restore generally uses the full plus intervening incrementals. Differentials capture changes since the full; restore generally uses the full plus the latest differential. Define RPO as the tolerable data-loss window and RTO as the target recovery time. Keep independent or immutable copies as required and time a sample restore.

21.2 • Data classification and disposition

Identify sensitive data before copying it. Follow approved retention, access, and transfer rules for personal, payment, or health records. For an incident, preserve evidence, document custody, and follow authorized collection steps. At disposal, use a policy-approved clear, purge, or destroy method matched to media and sensitivity. Ordinary file deletion or formatting is insufficient evidence of successful sanitization.

21.3 • Policy for AI-assisted support

An automated suggestion can be incomplete, biased, or wrong. Use approved services for permitted data only, redact identifying information, and verify claims against authoritative documentation or a known-good test. Public and privately managed services may have different access and retention terms; organizational policy determines what can be submitted.

Calculate the restore chain

A full backup ran Sunday at 18:00, with incrementals Monday and Tuesday at 18:00. A failure Wednesday at 11:00 means up to 17 hours of data loss if Tuesday’s copy is usable. Restore the Sunday full, Monday incremental, then Tuesday incremental. An eight-hour RPO would be missed even if the restore itself completes within the RTO.

Technician case file

Ransomware made a training share unavailable. A full backup and daily increments exist, and the newest backup may contain damaged files.

Write a short ticket in your study notebook. Include the evidence, your first action, and how you will verify the result. Use fictional details for this case.

Compare your ticket with the model response

Coordinate with incident response. Identify the last known-good restore point, restore into an isolated approved location, verify representative files and permissions, then document data loss, recovery duration, and acceptance.

Check that your ticket uses the scenario’s clues, explains the action in order, and specifies what a passing verification looks like.

Practice and readiness check

Choose the best answer for each question. Explain which clue supports your choice. The final question continues the technician case.

Sunday is full backup day. Monday and Tuesday are differential backups. What is needed to restore Tuesday?
Explanation

The latest differential contains changes made since the full.

A device is subject to an investigation hold. What is the next step before disposal?
Explanation

A hold requires preservation and documented handling.

A proposed summary includes customer identifiers and the tool is unapproved. What should you do?
Explanation

Tool approval and data-handling rules apply before sharing.

When validating the restored training share, what checks matter?
Explanation

Usability, access, and measured recovery outcomes are separate checks.
