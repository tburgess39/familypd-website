Your First Shift

Role: Junior IT Support Specialist. You are three minutes into your first shift when the first ticket arrives. Your job is not to randomly try fixes. Ask, observe, test, verify, and document.

Mission briefing

What does an IT specialist do?

The job changes from ticket to ticket, but strong technicians rely on the same core habits.

Problem solving

Identify what is actually wrong, gather evidence, isolate causes, and choose an appropriate response.

Communication

Listen carefully, ask useful questions, explain clearly, and document information so others can understand it.

Organization

Keep tools, parts, notes, and work areas organized so support work can be performed safely and efficiently.

Technical knowledge

Build broad knowledge across hardware, software, operating systems, networking, cloud technologies, security, and troubleshooting.

Technician method

Use a consistent troubleshooting process.

The instructor-provided CompTIA Module 1 material teaches a repeatable best-practice methodology for approaching unfamiliar problems.

Identify the problem

Gather exact symptoms and error messages. Ask who is affected, when it began, and what changed recently. Protect important data before risky repair work.

Establish a theory

Inspect the system, reproduce the issue when appropriate, consult documentation, and consider likely causes—including obvious ones.

Test the theory

Test one theory at a time so the evidence can confirm or reject your cause. If needed, create a new theory or escalate.

Plan and implement

Choose an authorized repair, replacement, or workaround. Consider policy, warranty, cost, risk, and the effect on the user.

Verify functionality

Confirm the original issue is resolved and the system is fully functional. Apply sensible preventive measures when appropriate.

Document

Record findings, actions, outcomes, tools or parts used, and lessons learned so the work can be understood later.

Question the obvious.

Power, cables, input sources, Wi-Fi switches, airplane mode, muted audio, and simple settings can create symptoms that look much more serious. Check simple, safe causes before replacing major hardware.

Ticket 001 — “The monitor is dead!”

MEDIUM

A teacher reports a completely black monitor. The desktop tower appears to be running.

Start with evidence and simple physical checks before replacing hardware.

Ticket 002 — “The internet is down!”

HIGH

One employee cannot reach a website. Nearby employees are working normally.

Because other users are working, isolate the problem to the affected client before assuming a network-wide outage.

Ticket 003 — “It became slow this morning.”

LOW

The PC was fine yesterday. The user installed a new application before leaving.

Recent changes are useful evidence. Test the most relevant theory before making unrelated changes.

Ticket 004 — “Nothing prints.”

MEDIUM

A user clicks Print, but no page appears. Other applications work normally.

Isolate whether the issue is the printer, connection, queue/service, or configuration before replacing anything.

Ticket 005 — Authorization Required

HIGH

You identify a failed component in a device that is under vendor warranty. Company policy says only the vendor may open it.

A professional technician respects authorization, company policy, warranty, and escalation boundaries.
