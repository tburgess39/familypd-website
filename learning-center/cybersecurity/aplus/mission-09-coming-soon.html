Mission goal

Set up mobile devices and peripherals, recognize wired and wireless connection methods, configure accounts and applications, identify laptop hardware, and troubleshoot power, physical, display, connectivity, and malware symptoms.

9.1 • Mobile devices are systems with many input/output options

Touch / pointing

Digitizer and gesture support, trackpad/touchpad, stylus.

Audio / video

Microphone, camera/webcam, speakers, headset.

Why it matters

A symptom may belong to the accessory, port, driver/configuration, or the device itself. Identify the component before replacing hardware.

Wired connection methods

The module includes USB-C, micro/mini USB, serial, HDMI, VGA, and Thunderbolt. A port replicator or docking station expands the connections available to a mobile computer.

A laptop needs more permanent desk connections for monitors, network, and peripherals. Which module category should you think about?

Port replicators and docking stations.

Wireless and location-related connections

TechnologyThink about

Troubleshooting habit: identify the radio/connection first. “Wireless” is not one technology.

Configure accounts and apps

Mobile setup can involve accounts, synchronization, permissions, application installation, and organization controls. The module's learning outcome is not only “connect the phone”—it is to configure the device so applications and services work as intended.

Synchronization

Think about what information must remain consistent across devices/services and whether the relevant account and sync settings are enabled.

Permissions

Applications may request access to hardware or data. A permissions change can affect whether an app can use resources such as network or hardware features.

Malware clue from the module: unexpected data collection/reporting, background processes, excess data transfer, hardware-permission changes, unauthorized resource access, or blocked app downloads can point toward malware.

9.3 • Laptop hardware: replace the right part

Laptops compress many desktop functions into a smaller chassis, so access and replacement procedures vary. Before opening a system, identify the failing component and understand how it connects.

Power

Battery and power-related components can create charging, runtime, and shutdown symptoms.

Display / input

Screen, backlight, digitizer, keyboard/touchpad, camera, microphone, and related connectors can fail independently.

Connectivity

Wireless hardware and antenna connections affect network behavior; physical ports can also be damaged.

Safety: the module specifically warns that a swollen battery can be a fire/explosion risk and should be removed immediately and disposed of properly.

The display image is visible but touch input no longer works after damage. Which component should be high on your list?

The digitizer, because the module associates digitizer damage with failed touch input.

A phone case is installed and touch sensitivity becomes poor. Must the digitizer be broken?

No. The module notes that screen protectors and phone cases can reduce sensitivity; test the simpler cause before replacing hardware.

Symptom sorter

Ask which layer of the mobile device best matches the clue before choosing a fix.

“Screen is dark, but device appears to be running.”

Think display/backlight before network.

“Touch does not register.”

Think digitizer/calibration before battery.

“Wi-Fi drops in one location.”

Think interference/connectivity before replacing the screen.

“Unexpected background data and permissions changed.”

Think malware indicators.

Mobile triage: POWER → PHYSICAL → DISPLAY/INPUT → CONNECTIVITY → SOFTWARE/MALWARE.

Interactive quick check

A device shows an image normally, but taps and swipes no longer register after screen damage.
