Did you know?

Technology gives you more ways to create than ever before.

01

You can build a real mobile app without typing traditional code.

Block-based tools let beginners connect visual pieces, test ideas, and make working apps.

02

A game can teach something important.

A fun game can help a younger child practice safety, money, spelling, feelings, routines, or decision-making.

03

Design is a career skill.

Posters, logos, videos, presentations, websites, and social graphics all use communication and design skills that organizations pay people to use.

04

Your family can be your first client.

Ask what is confusing, difficult, boring, unsafe, or disorganized at home. Then create something that makes it better.

05

AI should help you think—not replace your thinking.

Use it for ideas, explanations, outlines, feedback, code help, and practice. Check important facts and make the final choices yourself.

06

A small project can become a portfolio piece or business idea.

Save screenshots, drafts, feedback, and the final product. Explain the problem you solved and the skills you used.

From idea to finished project

Use this six-step creator path.

  1. 1
    Notice

    What could be safer, easier, more fun, more fair, or better organized?

  2. 2
    Ask

    What does your family need? Who will use your project?

  3. 3
    Learn

    Watch tutorials, use trusted sources, ask an adult, or practice with AI.

  4. 4
    Create

    Make a simple first version—even if it is rough.

  5. 5
    Test

    Let someone use it. Ask what worked and what was confusing.

  6. 6
    Improve & share

    Fix problems, credit sources, protect privacy, and add it to your portfolio.

Careers & entrepreneurship

Your project can help you discover what you might want to do in the future.

A family project can help you practice skills used in technology, business, health care, education, media, design, finance, engineering, public service, and many other careers.

  • Create a logo and product flyer.
  • Set a fair price and calculate costs.
  • Build a website or digital portfolio.
  • Practice a sales pitch or presentation.
  • Interview someone in a career you want to explore.
  • Turn a useful family solution into a service idea.
Portfolio challenge

Save proof of what you create.

Keep your idea, planning notes, drafts, screenshots, feedback, final product, and a short explanation of:

  • The problem you noticed
  • What you created
  • What tools and skills you used
  • What you would improve next

Create safely and responsibly

Protect yourself, your family, and the people your project is meant to help.

Protect privacy

Do not publish real addresses, phone numbers, passwords, school schedules, private family records, or personal medical and financial information.

Ask before publishing

Have a trusted adult review anything posted publicly or sent to an organization.

Credit sources

Say where facts, pictures, ideas, music, or tutorials came from. Do not claim someone else’s work as your own.

Check important facts

Health, legal, financial, school, safety, and community-resource information should be verified through trustworthy official sources.

Follow tool rules

Use age-appropriate accounts and follow family, school, platform, and copyright rules.

Build with—not just for—people

Ask intended users what they need. Test your project and listen respectfully to feedback.

Ready to make something?
