Kids & Teens

Turn FamilyPD ideas into student projects.

Young people can create apps, games, posters, videos, guides, surveys, trackers, or other projects connected to the five pillars and four guiding principles.

Project ideas

Choose a problem, question, or idea worth exploring.

Projects can be simple or advanced. The goal is to learn, create, and make something useful—not to complete a required blueprint.

Health

Health and safety projects

  • Create a family hydration or sleep tracker.
  • Design an emergency-preparation card game.
  • Build a digital-wellness quiz or screen-time reflection.
  • Create a healthy-routine poster or short video.
Relationships

Communication and connection projects

  • Create a kindness or appreciation challenge.
  • Design a conflict-repair comic or scenario game.
  • Build a communication-style quiz or conversation prompt tool.
  • Interview relatives and create a family-story project.
Education

Learning and career projects

  • Create flashcards, a study game, or homework organizer.
  • Build a career-exploration page or certification guide.
  • Create a school-terms dictionary for families.
  • Teach a technology, science, or cybersecurity topic.
Finances

Money and opportunity projects

  • Create a needs-versus-wants game.
  • Build a savings tracker or simple budget calculator.
  • Compare the cost of careers, training, or college options.
  • Create a family resource guide using official sources.
Goals

Planning and progress projects

  • Build a goal tracker, habit timer, or progress dashboard.
  • Create a procrastination game or motivation challenge.
  • Design a family calendar or reminder tool.
  • Create a vision-board template or reflection journal.
Community

Connection and service projects

  • Map local libraries, tutoring, food, health, or youth resources.
  • Create a volunteer-opportunity directory.
  • Build an outreach-message helper for families.
  • Interview a community organization and explain how it helps.
Systems & Policies

Household organization projects

  • Create a chore randomizer or workload visualizer.
  • Design a family-meeting agenda tool.
  • Create a household safety or technology-use policy template.
  • Build a checklist for routines, errands, or school preparation.
Self-Awareness

Reflection projects

  • Create a mood, stress, or gratitude tracker.
  • Design a strengths and interests survey.
  • Build a reflection journal or “what worked” check-in.
  • Create a project that helps people notice habits or patterns.

Resources

Tools students can use to learn and create.

ScratchGames, stories, animations, and beginner coding. Code.orgGuided computer-science lessons and projects. MIT App InventorBuild mobile apps with block-based coding. Canva for EducationPosters, presentations, videos, guides, and graphics. Khan AcademyLearn or review academic and life-skill topics. InstructablesStep-by-step project ideas and maker tutorials. FamilyPD Learning CenterExplore topics and source material connected to project ideas. FamilyPD Media BankReview visuals and infographics for inspiration.

Use safe, credible sources

Students should protect personal information, ask an adult before publishing or contacting organizations, credit original sources, and have an adult review health, legal, financial, or safety information before sharing it.
