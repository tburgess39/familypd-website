3. Build a support network before a crisis

Families do not have to manage every need alone. This is especially important for single parents, isolated caregivers, and households with no backup person.

Connection is part of family safety.

Social connection can support health and resilience. Building relationships before a crisis makes it easier to ask for transportation, childcare, meals, information, emotional support, or temporary relief when the family is under pressure (Office of the U.S. Surgeon General, 2023).

Fair does not always mean equal

Age, disability, health, work schedules, caregiving demands, and safety all matter. The calculator is designed to reveal patterns—not to shame anyone. Children can contribute through safe, developmentally appropriate tasks, while adults remain responsible for supervision and hazardous work.
