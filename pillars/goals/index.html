Pillar at a glance

Goals & Purpose: what families are building.

Turn values and needs into realistic priorities, visible plans, shared accountability, and measurable progress.

Includes

Personal and family goals, priorities, time use, planning, milestones, habits, motivation, discipline, reflection, accountability, and adjustment.

Strong looks like

Goals are written, connected to values, broken into manageable steps, reviewed consistently, supported by systems, and adjusted without abandoning the purpose.

Watch for

Too many goals, vague intentions, repeated postponement, dependence on motivation, no calendar or milestones, perfectionism, or failure being treated as final.

First family system

Choose one personal goal and one family goal for the next 30–90 days. Name the next action, owner, due date, support needed, and review date.

05Direction

Why this pillar matters

Purpose gives the other four pillars direction, while consistent review turns intention into progress.

Goal-setting research emphasizes clarity, appropriate challenge, commitment, feedback, and the complexity of the task. “If-then” implementation intentions—deciding in advance when, where, and how to act—can improve follow-through (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006; Locke & Latham, 2002).

FamilyPD principle

Use evidence to understand the situation, then choose a family practice that can be repeated—not a label that creates shame.

What this pillar includes

A practical whole-family view.

Purpose and values

Why the goal matters and how it connects to the person or family you want to become.

Clear outcomes

A result that can be described, measured, and recognized.

Action systems

Small steps, routines, calendars, support, and “if-then” plans.

Review and adjustment

Feedback, reflection, celebration, problem-solving, and permission to revise the plan.

The framework at work

Build goals through all four guiding principles.

Information creates awareness. FamilyPD turns that awareness into household practices that can be repeated, discussed, and improved.

Notice the pattern

Signs this pillar may need attention

These are discussion signals, not diagnoses or proof that a family is failing.

  • The family has many wishes but no written priorities or next actions.
  • Goals are repeatedly set without considering health, time, money, or support.
  • A missed day or setback is treated as total failure.
  • No one reviews progress until a deadline or crisis arrives.
  • Family members are working hard but cannot explain what the work is moving toward.

FamilyPD perspective

Begin with self-awareness. Choose one system. Clarify one policy. Reach for community support when the family needs knowledge, services, or accountability.

Build a family system

Small practices that make the pillar visible.

Choose what fits your household. A useful system is simple enough to repeat and flexible enough to improve.

1

One-season focus

Choose one or two priorities instead of trying to transform everything at once.

2

Goal statement

Write the outcome, why it matters, how progress will be measured, and a realistic review date.

3

Next-action list

Break the goal into actions small enough to schedule.

4

If-then plan

Decide: “If this obstacle happens, then we will take this backup action.”

5

Progress check

Review what worked, what did not, and what needs to change during family meetings.

6

Visible wins

Track completed actions and celebrate progress without waiting for the final finish line.

Family meeting

Talk about it together.

  1. What matters most to us in this season?
  2. Which goal would create the greatest relief or opportunity?
  3. What is the smallest useful next action?
  4. What obstacle is most likely, and what is our if-then plan?
  5. How will we know when to continue, adjust, pause, or complete the goal?

Use age-appropriate language. People may pass, ask for time, or share privately when a topic feels sensitive.

Family challenge

Turn insight into one repeatable action.

Choose one personal or family goal. Write the outcome, one measure, the next action, and an if-then plan for the most likely obstacle. Review it during the next family meeting.
