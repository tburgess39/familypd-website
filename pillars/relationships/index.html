Pillar Two

Relationships

Build connection, trust, boundaries, and repair on purpose.

Relationships shape the emotional climate of the home and influence how family members communicate, learn, recover from conflict, and reach for support.

Start a family check-inReview sources

What research suggests

Use information to guide action—not to judge the family.

Social connection is associated with better health and well-being, while responsive back-and-forth interactions support children’s development. Regular, open conversations and active listening help strengthen parent-child relationships (National Scientific Council on the Developing Child, 2004; Office of the Surgeon General, 2023; CDC, 2024).

What this pillar includes

A practical whole-family view.

Connection

Consistent attention, shared time, emotional availability, and a sense of belonging.

Communication

Active listening, clear requests, respectful tone, and room for questions.

Boundaries

Knowing what is acceptable, what is not, and what action follows when a boundary is crossed.

Repair

Owning harm, apologizing, making amends, rebuilding trust, and changing patterns.

Notice the pattern

Signs this pillar may need attention

These are discussion signals, not diagnoses or proof that a family is failing.

  • Most conversations happen only when someone is in trouble or a crisis occurs.
  • Family members feel mocked, dismissed, unsafe, or unable to disagree respectfully.
  • One person carries nearly all emotional and household labor.
  • Conflict is avoided completely or repeatedly escalates without repair.
  • The family is isolated and has few trusted people or organizations to call for support.

FamilyPD perspective

Begin with self-awareness. Choose one system. Clarify one policy. Reach for community support when the family needs knowledge, services, or accountability.

Build a family system

Small practices that make the pillar visible.

Choose what fits your household. A useful system is simple enough to repeat and flexible enough to improve.

1

Connection ritual

Protect a repeatable time for conversation, meals, play, calls, or check-ins.

2

Family meeting norms

Agree to listen without interruption, use respectful language, stay on topic, and end with next steps.

3

Repair process

Use a shared pattern: name what happened, listen to impact, apologize, decide what changes, and follow up.

4

Boundary language

Practice short statements such as “I can help with ___, but I cannot ___.”

5

Recognition habit

Name effort, growth, kindness, and contributions—not only errors.

6

Relationship map

Identify close supports, community connections, mentors, and relationships the family wants to strengthen.

Family meeting

Talk about it together.

  1. When do we feel most connected as a family?
  2. What makes it easier—or harder—to talk honestly in our home?
  3. Is there an apology, thank-you, or clarification that needs to happen?
  4. Which responsibilities or emotional tasks need to be shared more fairly?
  5. Who outside our household is part of our healthy support system?

Use age-appropriate language. People may pass, ask for time, or share privately when a topic feels sensitive.

Family challenge

Turn insight into one repeatable action.

Choose one relationship practice—ten minutes of device-free conversation, specific praise, a repair conversation, or a shared activity—and make it predictable enough that family members can count on it.
